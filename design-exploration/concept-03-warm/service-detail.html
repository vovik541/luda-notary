Notarizing a power of attorney

You have the document ready and need the signature notarized. Here is exactly what happens, and what I can and cannot do.

What I do

  • Check the ID of the person giving the power
  • Make sure they understand and sign willingly
  • Complete the California acknowledgment
  • Take a thumbprint in my journal, as California requires for powers of attorney

What I cannot do

  • Choose which power of attorney you need
  • Write or change the wording
  • Explain its legal effect

An attorney can help with those questions.

The appointment

At the signer's home, another agreed place, or my office in Citrus Heights.

  1. Tell me what you need

    Call, text or send the form with the document type, the number of signers and where you would like to meet.

  2. We confirm the details

    I confirm the time, the place and the full fee. Nothing is booked until we both agree.

  3. We meet and sign

    Bring every signer, an acceptable ID for each one and the complete, unsigned document.

  4. Pay and you are done

    Pay by Zelle, cash or check. I complete my journal entry and the certificate on the spot.
